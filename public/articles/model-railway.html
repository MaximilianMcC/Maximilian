Model railway

One of my major hobbies is modelling British railways in OO gauge, which is in 1:76.2 scale. This means one metre is scaled down to 13.12 millimetres. This is the most popular scale for British modelling. I use the free version of AnyRail for all my track planning (it's very good) And I use Blender for planning all the terrain and structures and stuff.

This article has both the history of my previous layouts, and my current layout. If you'd like to skip directly to my current layout, and list of stock, then click here.

The beginning

Ever since I first watched Thomas, I've wanted a model railway. I kinda instantly fell in love with how amazing the models used for the filming of Thomas were. I especially liked the style of the HiT era. Thomas from 2004-2008 Growing up I spent most of my time playing with TrackMaster: a range of motorised thomas toys. Over the years I amassed an incredibly large collection of it. Once every few months my parents would allow me to take over the lounge and dining room to make a layout that would pretty much take up the whole of the house. As I got a little older I began to start taking my layout-planning seriously. I would design realistic layouts and I operated them very strictly. I know it is pretty cringe for a 19-year-old to be glazing a bit of children's media, but I love Thomas so much and its something I'm no longer embarrassed to admit.

Here are some examples of the incredible TTTE sets. I found these all in like 10 minutes but if I do a rewatch I will definitely get some better examples. The whole show looks so pretty; I could find a thousand more shots. The way it was modelled was/is a major inspiration to me. I wish I could go to Sodor. The fictional island is situated in the middle of the Isle of Man and England.

My first 'real' layout

In 2017 I got my first train set: the Hornby The most 'popular' manufacturer of British model trains Country Flyer. It came with one of those naff standard Hornby 0-4-0s, 0-4-0 is the wheel arrangement of the train. It means there are zero wheels in the back, four driven/powered wheels in the middle (two on each side), and zero in the front. an open wagon, and a 4-wheel compartment coach. Also a loop of track. With this being a train set intended for beginners, none of the stuff in it was designed to be prototypical. Accurate to the irl prototype (real and not made up) I also got a few of the Hornby TrakMat extension packs. Looking back at it, it's all pretty rubbish, but to 11 year old me this was awesome.

Model trains are extremely expensive ordinarily, and that is made worse with my living in New Zealand. At school, in year 7, we had to do some sort of 'project' (I have forgotten all details about it) and I decided to make a model railway. By deciding to make a model railway for school, I was able to get my parents to agree to buy me all the stuff.

The layout was really crap. I used a giant bit of polystyrene for the base, which was spray-painted brown. It was covered in holes from the paint melting it away. With it being made from polystyrene, the ends sagged down and little balls fell off it all the time. It was so grim but I loved it very much.

Some second-hand track and buildings were purchased from TradeMe. New Zealand eBay I also got three tri-ang open wagons and two brake vans (one LMS and one BR). I didn't really end up using most of the buildings (they came in a lot of like 25), or the track, but I loved the wagons.

I had this layout for about a year, but it didn't really get much use. I quickly grew bored of it, and I also didn't have any money to do anything with it.

A train set for Christmas

I reckon there is no Christmas present more stereotypical than a train set. Either 2018 or 2019 Christmas (I've forgotten) I got my second train set: The Hornby East Coast Express. I wanted this so badly; I remember watching just about every review on YouTube about it. I have a very vivid memory of me setting it up in the conservatory on Christmas day whilst everyone was having lunch or something. Having a proper tender engine was so awesome in comparison to the rinky little tank engine I was previously using. My absolute favourite part of it was watching its valve gear The connecting rods that move the wheels on a steam locomotive: at slow speed.

My current layout

In 2024 I fell back in-love with the railway again. I was really itching to do some shunting, Moving around bits of rolling stock into a specific order so I pulled out some of the old manky track and made an inglenook A popular shunting puzzle where you build a train of five using three sidings layout in my bedroom on the carpet. At this time I started to watch heaps of modelling videos, and a few episodes of TTTE, which got me super inspired to start a proper railway. For real this time.

At this time I was really interested in filthy industrial layouts, so I brought a few Metcalfe Extremely popular brand that makes card model kits retaining walls. Making them was so fun, and a Metcalfe kit was something I had always wanted to try.

I went around my house and started measuring up spots for a model railway to go. Model railways are massive (much larger than what I think you're imagining) Because of the size, I decided to make an end-to-end A layout where there is no loop for continuous running. Once the train gets to the end, it must reverse back the other way. shunting layout. On 9 August, 2024, I went to Bunnings Australian / New Zealand Hardware store and brought a heap of wood with my Dad, which we used to construct a proper baseboard.

I designed a track plan, then I ordered all of the needed track. Shipping between England and New Zealand is never fast, but it took an extremely long time for my stuff to arrive. So much so that I emailed the company that I brought the track from. They said that apparently my order had been lost in the Royal Mail facility, meaning it never even left Britain. They replicated my order and sent it to me again. Some time later, both of my orders arrived. This was fantastic, because now I was able to make a more complicated layout with all this new 'free' track. On 17 October, 2024, I laid the track for the first time.

On my 18th birthday I got some money, which I immediately blew on new rolling stock. This was also my first of many Rails Rails of Sheffield is the most popular retailer for British model railway stuff. orders. I got both a GWR Great Western Railway and an LMS London Midland and Scottish Railway vent van, a GWR 5-plank open wagon, and an unpainted 7-plank open wagon. All of this stuff was Dapol. A Welsh model railway manufacturer who mainly specialise in 'cheap' rolling stock It was so amazing to finally get some proper stock to run. With these new wagons, I was able to use the Inglenook in my layout. You need eight wagons to properly operate the inglenook sidings.

I tried to add in point motors but it did not work out well. At all. I would rather not write about it because it still frustrates me to this day. Maybe my next layout will have them. Hopefully I will have no part in them.

The first standalone locomotive that I brought was the Metropolitan Railways E class by Rapido trains UK. It is such a lovely model (and locomotive). I actually saw it irl, but it was being overhauled so it was lowk a ship of theseus moment. Right now I have pre-ordered the Metropolitan Railway Evolution coaches from Rapido, which I am extremely excited to get. Right now I run it with blood & custard Mark 1s. The Metropolitan E class used to run on suburban/commuter lines of what is now the London Overground and Tube. Imagine how cool it would have been to see this thing in the tube. Once she is overhauled I am 100% going to see her again (properly this time)

England dlc

To celebrate the 200th anniversary of the modern railway, I went to England. You can read about it in my Railway200 article. Most of the railways I visited in England were having galas, Special events where they run more trains than usual. They often have touring locomotives from other railways visit, meaning regulars can see something new. which meant they had the little vendor stalls selling models — many second-hand. Buying stuff second-hand is a massive part of the model railway hobby, and it's something I don't get to do (New Zealand) When I came back home my suitcase was literally bursting. I purchased one ven van, one salt van, seven tankers, and six open wagons. I also got a Hornby Peckett, Look at how pretty this is.
